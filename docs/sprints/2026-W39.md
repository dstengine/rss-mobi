## What we learned

- **Most originals are already in Google.** 299 of 335 checked posts
  (89%) were, so each open page costs about nine paid checks.
- **Feed pages win "<site> rss feed" queries.** All three queries with
  impressions were of that shape, and 7 of the top 10 pages by impressions
  are `/feed/<slug>/`; the site averaged position 5.3.
- **A third of submissions end in an error, and half of those are ours.**
  6 `submit_error` against 18 `submit_start`; 11 succeeded (61%). Three
  were 422 (not a feed) and three 429 — our own limit of five
  submissions an hour per address. At this traffic a 429 is most likely
  one person seeding feeds, but it is a refusal a real submitter would
  see too.
- **The front page is a third of all views and the submit page is next.**
  134 of 424 views (32%) are the front page, 38 the submit form; feed
  pages had 57 views for 9 follows and 9 outbound clicks. Post pages had
  9 views — they are not in search yet (below).
- **The reader is opened but barely read in.** 31 reader views, 2
  `read_post`, 0 `reader_more`.
- **DataForSEO spend runs at the cap every day.** $0.12 is used up by
  about 00:22 UTC — roughly 40 checks a day — and the queue grew from 392
  on 28 September to 446 by that evening: new posts from non-seeded feeds
  arrive faster than the cap lets them be checked. The week's 319 checks
  include 24 September, before the cap ($0.597).
- **Post pages are not indexed yet.** 0 of 36 open pages and 0 of 430
  sitemap URLs in Google after four days. Too early to call; it is the
  number to watch.
- **The cloaking test can fail without cloaking.** One run in five saw
  different bytes for the two user agents on the uncached search page;
  twelve paired fetches and three more runs matched. The likeliest cause
  is data changing between two renders, but the test cannot tell that
  apart from a real difference.

## What changes

- The cloaking test should tell a moving page from a split one: fetch
  each user agent twice and fail only when the difference repeats.
- The retro reads `metrics_daily` now (#9): views by page type, error
  codes and subscribe targets go into the table every week.
- Decide the DataForSEO cap against the queue: at 40 checks a day either
  the cap rises, or fewer posts are queued (for example only the newest
  per feed).
- `submit-steps-v1` is inactive, so its "preview" rows are the control
  for everyone, not an experiment readout.
- Next retro: first real week-on-week comparison; also compare open pages
  against pages in Google.
